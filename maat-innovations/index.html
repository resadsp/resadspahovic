The problem

A clinic run in four places at once.

Appointments in one program, the laboratory in another, finance in a spreadsheet and stock in a notebook. Each does its part, but nothing joins up on its own.

The patient leaves without paying

They will settle up when they collect the results. They come back three weeks later, a different shift is on, and nobody knows what is still owed — or whether anything is.

Results are chased by phone

A patient calls to ask whether the report is ready. Nobody knows if it was sent, to whom, or when — so it goes out twice, or never.

Doctor earnings from memory

At month end someone adds it up in a spreadsheet, estimating what went on materials and what the doctor is owed. It never matches what everyone assumed.

Stock in a notebook

What was used, where and by whom — nobody knows until the stocktake, when it is too late to put anything right.

What changes

Less searching, less retyping, fewer missed payments.

Every feature exists because something kept getting stuck in a clinic's day — and now it doesn't.

No examination left unbilled

A finished but unpaid examination does not slip by: the front desk sees it listed with how many days it is overdue, management sees it as an alert.

Appointments never overlap

Every service has its own duration, so the program offers only times that are truly free — to the patient online and to the front desk on the phone.

A laboratory without retyping

A barcode on every tube; analyser results land in the order on their own, and the technician only checks and confirms them.

A fiscal receipt for every payment

Part payments too, in the laboratory too, and at the patient's home. A receipt that was not issued stays listed until it is.

A waiting room that calls patients itself

A TV in the waiting room calls the patient to the room by name. The doctor holds the call or calls early with one click.

Management sees the clinic in numbers

Revenue by department, doctor and service, doctors' fees, stock and alerts — every morning, without copying into spreadsheets.

What it covers

Twenty modules, one system.

Every workstation sees what it needs, and nothing is entered twice. A small practice uses the first few; a clinic with a laboratory and a theatre uses all of them.

Scheduling

Online, by phone or at the front desk. The patient picks the service and the slot lasts as long as that service takes — computed from the doctor's real schedule and theatre availability.

Clinical work

A chart across every department, ICD-10 diagnoses, therapy, follow-up and referrals between doctors. The report comes out as a PDF with letterhead, stamp and signature — or on a screen in the consulting room.

Specialist reports

Structured forms where a blank page is not enough: cardiac ultrasound with 47 measurements, gynaecology and pregnancy, vein mapping of both legs entered by the technician who did the scan — with the images attached.

Laboratory

A catalogue of 1,200+ tests with its own price list, a barcode label per tube and analyser, results with reference ranges — values from the analyser arrive as a proposal the technician confirms.

Surgical unit

Theatre scheduling, with anaesthetist clearance required before a patient is admitted.

Home visits

Field orders, technician dispatch, therapy taken from the patient's own report at booking, materials from stock or a contracted pharmacy, and call-out settlement.

Billing

Part payments, cash and card on the same invoice, discounts, and debt tracked over years. Money never passes through the system — the clinic takes payment at its own till and the system keeps the record.

Fiscal receipts

Two registers — front desk and laboratory. A receipt for every payment, instalments and doorstep collections included; a refund is its own document.

Till by shift

Each shift takes over the till by counting it and hands it over the same way. A difference is never corrected quietly — it needs a note. The change float never counts as revenue.

Stock

Inventory and goods receipt, with automatic deduction on every service — and the name of who took it out.

Management and finance

Revenue by source, performance and earnings by doctor, fixed costs, pharmacy purchases as costs, and break-even per service.

Equipment

Service and calibration with due dates, depreciation, and return per device.

Quality

Incidents and corrective actions, laboratory quality control. The log cannot be deleted — auditors ask for it.

Calls and waiting list

Call log, missed calls, how many calls turn into appointments, and the waiting list.

Waiting-room screen

A TV in the waiting room calls the patient to the room the moment it frees up. Shared rooms, a “hold” button for the doctor, first name and initial only — and no ticket machine.

Marketing

What each channel brings in and what one new patient costs — from actual revenue, not estimates.

Patient portal

Patients see their appointments, results and images, download the PDF and book themselves — with two-factor sign-in. Reminders go only with their consent, which they can withdraw at any time.

Clinic website

The price list, departments and doctors on the clinic's own website come straight from the system — a price changed in the app is the price on the site.

Command centre

Alerts in one place and a morning report: what stalled yesterday and what needs a decision today.

Procurement

Suppliers, orders, and the make-or-buy sum for whether to run a test in house or send it out.

Integrations and compliance

The parts you do not get to opt out of.

Fiscal receipts, national e-health export, insurance checks, laboratory analysers. All of it is built and working — the readiness status is stated plainly below.

Fiscal receipts and e-invoicing

Two registers — front desk and laboratory. A receipt for every payment, instalments and collections at the patient's door included; a refund is its own document, and a payment without a receipt stays listed until it gets one. E-invoices for corporate clients.

E-health (FHIR)

Data export in FHIR format, which national systems and other providers can read.

Insurance check

Coverage is checked before the appointment, so nothing is charged that the insurer would have paid.

Laboratory analysers

Two protocols, ASTM and HL7 (MLLP). A bridge beside the analyser keeps every result on disk until the server confirms it — nothing is lost when the internet drops, and no port is opened into the clinic. Results land in the order without retyping.

StatusAll four connections are built and run in sandbox mode; the analyser links are tested against simulated instruments over a real network connection. Going live is configuration — endpoint, key and certificate — not a rewrite. If configuration is missing, the system will not quietly fall back to simulation: it raises an error, so an invoice is never shown as fiscalised when it is not.
Security and confidentiality

Medical data stays confidential.

Health data is the most sensitive data a clinic holds. So it is protected at every step, not just by a password.

Encrypted at rest

Names, addresses, contacts, diagnoses and laboratory results are stored encrypted — anyone who gets hold of the database alone gets no names, no diagnoses and no phone numbers.

Everyone sees their own

Rights by role: the lab technician does not see finance, the front desk cannot change doctors' reports, a patient sees only their own appointments and results.

Two-factor sign-in

A code from an app on the phone alongside the password, and automatic sign-out when the program is idle.

Access trail on charts

Every chart opened leaves a record: who, what and when. A report can be written only once the appointment has started; opening it earlier takes a written reason, which stays in the trail. Patient reviews are visible to management only.

Backups

They run on their own — encrypted, the database and attachments together — and stay with you. A restore does not touch existing data until the copy has been verified.

The data is yours

The system is installed on your server and your domain, and stays there. We do not hold your database or a copy of your data.

UpdatesOver 2,500 automated checks run before any change ships, and each run counts every table before and after, so testing leaves no data behind. That does not mean bugs do not exist — it means work done for one clinic does not break what already works at another.
